# Global optimization of Savonius-type vertical axis wind turbine with multiple circular-arc blades using validated 3D CFD model

Ivo Marinić-Kragić, Damir Vučina, Zoran Milas

## Abstract

Vertical-axis wind turbines, such as Savonius-type wind turbines (SWT), are an attractive choice for small-scale electricity generation. Some of the advantages of the SWTs are low speed, low noise, and self-starting ability, but the main disadvantage is the low efficiency. In this paper, the efficiency is improved by optimization of a multi-blade SWT (2, 4 and 6), with blades based on circular arc segments. Additional optimization variables were the tip-speed ratio and the aspect ratio. The main novelty of this paper is the global optimization process using genetic algorithm, which analyzed many different designs (~10^5) using a validated 3D CFD model with 4eq SST turbulence model. As large number of designs was investigated, the result may be considered as the global optimal solution. In the 4 and 6-blade cases, the global optimal solution was similar to the previously proposed Scooplet-based design. The design was improved to power coefficient cP=0.32 with aspect ratio 1.5, and additionally to cP=0.34 with increased aspect ratio. For the 2-blade case, the obtained design was significantly different than the classical SWT and achieved 12% relative improvement.

Keywords: Shape optimization; Vertical axis wind turbine; Savonius wind turbine; Global optimization

## 1 Introduction

Savonius wind turbine rotor is simple in design and can be easily fabricated. SWT operation is independent of wind direction, making a yaw system needless. SWT has high starting-torque capability. The low optimal SWT tip-speed ratio, TSR 0.7-1.1, produces lower rotational speed than a same-size HAWT, for which the stated optimal TSR is 6-7. SWT peak power coefficient is reported as about 0.2 for a classical Savonius rotor, while modifications have raised it close to 0.3 while preserving simple design and high starting ability.

SWT modifications include blade shape; blade arrangement, including overlap, spacing, tilting, spiraling, and multistaging; and multi-blade design. Stationary augmenters can significantly increase power coefficient, but at the expense of directional insensitivity and simplicity. The paper notes that 2D CFD resembles mid-plane flow in a rotor with AR tending to infinity and generally over-predicts SWT cP; 3D CFD with the same turbulence model can under-predict it. The current study globally optimizes 2-, 4-, and 6-blade circular-arc configurations with a validated 3D CFD model.

### 2.2 CFD model

SWT modeling uses transient Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes equations. The paper used the 4-equation transitional SST turbulence model. One half of the rotor was modeled using a symmetry condition at the rotor mid-plane. The rotating zone had diameter `1.8d`, where `d = 0.68 m`. Overall domain dimensions were `5d` in z, `40d` in x, and `30d` in y. The rotor axis was `15d` from the inlet and `25d` from the outlet. A `3d`-wide high-resolution wake region began `2d` downstream of the rotor axis and continued to the outlet. The initial condition used `v∞ = 12 m/s` and `omega = 31.4 rad/s`, corresponding to TSR `0.9`; TSR varied during optimization. A sliding-mesh interface joined rotating and stationary zones, outlet pressure was zero, and symmetry conditions were used at the lower mid-plane, upper plane, and lateral sides.

![Figure 2: Computational zones](../images/va29-fig2.jpg)

The high-resolution mesh used a thin structured prismatic layer around blade walls: 20 normal layers and a first-layer thickness of `0.01 mm` for `y+ ≈ 1`. Blade-edge, blade, stator, high-resolution-wake, and outer-stator element sizes were `0.5 mm`, `5 mm`, `13 mm`, `125 mm`, and `600 mm`, respectively. Size transitions used growth rate `1.1`; the height used 20 elements.

### 2.3 Grid independency study

For the classical turbine, meshes from 175,119 to 1,586,786 elements and timesteps from 4 degrees to 0.5 degrees produced cP from 0.2201 to 0.2168. The complex six-blade study produced cP values of 0.3233, 0.3199, 0.3179, 0.3145, and 0.3143 as resolution increased; the difference between the final two meshes was 1%. The authors used the highest-resolution mesh for results and expected approximately 1% mesh-related error. They used a two-revolution average for cP because the moving average had negligible variation.

### 2.4 CFD model validation

The CFD model was validated against experimental power-coefficient data for a semicircular rotor with overlap ratio `s/d = 0.1`, and against local pressure-coefficient differences from a second experiment. For the local validation case, the rotor had `s/d = 0.15`, `AR = 1`, `D = 300 mm`, `4 mm` blade thickness, `v∞ = 6 m/s`, and `TSR = 0.9`. The paper states that no tunnel-blockage correction was applied to those measurements. The 4-equation transitional SST model predicted the integral data marginally better than the two-equation SST model, though all models slightly under-predicted the experimental cP values.

## 3 Shape Parameterization and Optimization

### 3.1 Shape parameterization

The study parameterized blades using up to three circular arcs. Up to three blades, `c1`, `c2`, and `c3`, were independent; the remaining blades repeated these shapes after 180-degree rotation. An individual blade had up to five parameters: x-y coordinates of both blade edges and curvature radius, parameterized as blade thickness `mi`. One point was fixed at the constrained outer rotor radius. A constraint prevented intersecting blades, though blades could be arbitrarily close.

Figure 7. Shape parameterization composed of circular arcs.

![Figure 7: Shape parameterization](../images/va29-fig7.jpg)

### 3.2 Optimization procedure

The objective was average power coefficient. The 2-, 4-, and 6-blade cases had 4, 9, and 14 optimization variables, respectively, including TSR. ModeFrontier implemented the NSGA-II genetic algorithm. Eight PCs ran 18 CFD simulations in parallel. Sobol sequences generated initial populations of 50, 500, and 2,000 designs for the 2-, 4-, and 6-blade cases. The authors reran the optimization with varying initial populations; reducing initial population could prevent reaching the reported optimum.

## 4 Results and Discussion

### 4.1 Overall results

All reported results used the same 3D CFD model, 500,000 elements, and a 0.5-degree equivalent timestep. Aspect ratio was initially `1.5`. The optimized two-blade design increased cP by 12% relative to the classical `s/d = 0.1` design and selected TSR `1.2`. The optimized six-blade case produced the highest cP, but only negligibly more than the four-blade case; the authors therefore concluded that further blade-number increases are unlikely to improve performance. The six-blade improvement over the classical design was 48%.

### 4.2 Optimum for a 2-blade design

The two-blade optimum had cP `0.242`, compared with `0.217` for the classical design under the same CFD model. Its overlap was practically zero rather than the typical `s/d ≈ 0.1`, and its optimized TSR was `1.2`. The study reports little performance change across TSR 1.1-1.3 and approximately 10 mm geometric variation.

### 4.3 Optimum for a 4-blade design

The four-blade optimum achieved cP `0.318`. Its main blades were flatter than the earlier design optimized with 2D CFD; under matched 3D CFD and aspect ratio, the new shape improved cP by 3%. The paper reports three-dimensional heightwise power-contribution changes, including off-mid-plane maxima for the optimized four- and six-blade designs.

### 4.5 Optimal aspect ratio

For all cases, cP was near its maximum around `AR = 3`; further increases had negligible benefit. The six-blade cP increased from `0.32` to nearly `0.34`. From AR `1` to `2.5`, cP increased 15% for the four- and six-blade designs, compared with 5% for the two-blade design. The paper states cP drops significantly for all designs below `AR = 1`.

## 5 Conclusions

The study used a validated 3D CFD model and global genetic optimization of circular-arc Savonius rotors with 2, 4, and 6 blades. It states that its large evaluation count, approximately 10^5 designs, was needed for blade-shape convergence. The two-blade design reached TSR `1.2` and a 12% cP improvement over the classical design. The four- and six-blade results were similar to a scooplet-based configuration; additional aspect-ratio consideration reached cP `0.34`, almost 50% above the classical design. The study recommends exploring elliptical or more general spline/surface parameterizations in future work.
