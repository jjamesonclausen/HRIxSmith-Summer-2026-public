# Review of Straight Bladed Vertical Axis Wind Turbine

Prakhar Dubel, Amit Ray Chowdhary, and Alok Verma

## Abstract
This paper studies straight-bladed vertical axis wind turbine (SB-VAWT) blade materials. The authors argue that blades must be produced at moderate cost, last through the predicted lifetime, and remain competitive in price. Aluminum blades made by extrusion and bending are common, but aluminum alloy has poor fatigue properties and its allowable stress decreases with repeated cyclic loading.

## 1. Introduction
Straight-bladed vertical axis wind turbine (SB-VAWT) is the simplest type of turbomachine and is mechanically easy to construct. The key design parameter for a cost-effective SB-VAWT is blade material. The authors argue that blades should be produced at moderate cost, last 20 to 30 years, and remain competitive in price.

Compared with HAWTs, fixed-pitch SB-VAWTs are mechanically simpler and do not require yaw mechanics, pitch control, or wind-direction sensing. Many components needing maintenance are located at ground level, which simplifies maintenance. The authors also note that aluminum blades made by extrusion and bending are the most common VAWT material, but aluminum alloy has poor fatigue properties and allowable stress decreases with repeated cyclic stress applications. The paper therefore investigates alternative materials for SB-VAWT blades.

## 2. Required Properties of the Blade Materials
SB-VAWT blades are exposed to diversified load conditions and dynamic stresses that are more severe than many mechanical applications. Based on operational conditions, blade materials should:

- have adequately high yield strength for longer life
- endure a very large number of fatigue cycles during service
- have high stiffness to maintain aerodynamic performance
- have low density to reduce gravity and normal-force components
- be corrosion resistant
- be suitable for cheaper fabrication methods
- be efficiently manufactured into their final form
- provide long-term mechanical performance per unit cost

Fatigue is the major problem facing both HAWTs and VAWTs. An operating turbine may be exposed to more than 10^8 cycles during a 30-year lifetime.

## 3. Prospective Materials
Smaller wind turbine blades are usually made of aluminum or laminated wood. Metals were initially popular because they yield low-cost blades and can be manufactured reliably, but most metallic blades, such as steel, are relatively heavy. Laminated wood was tried on early machines in 1977. The most popular materials for different wind turbines are wood, aluminum, and fiberglass composites.

### 3.1 Wood and Wood Epoxy
Wood is a naturally occurring composite material with good fatigue properties, high strength-to-weight ratio, good stiffness, and good resilience. Wood and wood-epoxy blades have been used extensively for small and medium HAWTs. The main limitation is moisture stability, though this can be controlled with good design and quality manufacturing. Large blades are also hindered by joining efficiency.

### 3.2 Aluminum
Aluminum blades fabricated by extrusion and bending are common VAWT materials. Early Darrieus blades were made from stretched and formed steel sheets or from aluminum alloy extrusions combined with fiberglass. The paper notes that formed steel sheets were difficult to shape into smooth airfoils, while the aluminum/fiberglass combination was expensive. Aluminum alloy still suffers from poor fatigue properties and reduced allowable stress under cyclic loading.

### 3.3 Fiberglass Composites
Fiberglass-reinforced composites are currently the blade materials of choice for HAWTs. These materials, also called fiber-reinforced plastics, are usually made with E-glass in polyester, vinyl ester, or epoxy matrices and are often produced with hand-layup techniques. Resin transfer molding and pultrusion have improved quality and reduced manufacturing cost.

Fiberglass composites and wood/epoxy are attractive because they have low density, good mechanical properties, excellent corrosion resistance, tailorable material properties, and versatile fabrication methods. The paper also notes that an extensive database for fiberglass composites helps designers improve performance without significantly increasing costs.

## 4. Literature Survey
The cited studies include:

- shaft diameter effects on Darrieus turbine performance
- an experimental study of a combined three-bucket H-rotor with Savonius turbine
- a performance study of a three-bladed airfoil-shaped H-rotor made from FRP
- VAWT performance prediction using high- and low-fidelity analysis
- optimization of a VAWT micro turbine for low tip-speed-ratio operation

## 5. Conclusions
The paper identifies required properties for SB-VAWT blade materials, compares available materials by mechanical properties and cost, and concludes that pultruded FRP is a prospective alternative blade material. The design analysis reported in the paper shows pultruded FRP to be superior to conventionally used aluminum.
