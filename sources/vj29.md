# A comprehensive review on H-type Darrieus wind turbine: aerodynamics, blade profile, CFD simulations

Venaganti Abhinaya and G Ganesh Kumar

https://doi.org/10.1186/s44147-026-00968-x

## Abstract

The increasing global need for clean and sustainable energy has led to a surge in research on efficient wind energy conversion systems. The H-type Darrieus wind turbine has drawn a lot of interest among vertical axis wind turbines (VAWTs) because of its straightforward design, acceptance of omnidirectional winds, and adaptability to low-wind and urban settings. The aerodynamic performance of H-type Darrieus wind turbines are thoroughly examined in this review, with particular attention paid to important design elements such blade profile, pitch angle, solidity, number of blades, and tip speed ratio (TSR). The impact of flow separation, vortex formation, and dynamic stall on turbine efficiency is also highlighted in the review. In order to comprehend flow behaviour and optimize performance, both experimental studies and numerical simulations, including those based on computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and blade element momentum (BEM) theory are covered. Additionally examined are recent developments in passive and active flow control methodologies. In addition to identifying future research directions for improving the efficiency and applicability of H-type Darrieus turbines, this study attempts to provide a comprehensive understanding of the aerodynamic factors influencing their performance.

Keywords: TSR, Lift Force, Wind Energy, Airfoil, Flow control

## Genesis of the Problem and Novelty of the Study
Wind energy systems have attracted a lot of interest due to the worldwide focus on
sustainable energy. Although horizontal axis wind turbines (HAWTs) dominate the
commercial setup, vertical axis wind turbines (VAWTs), specifically that of H-type
Darrieus type, have several exclusive benefits, which include ability to accept winds in
all directions, low noises, small foot-print design and ease of maintenance. They are

production in urban and offshore settings. But they have not been successfully deployed
in practice because of inherent aerodynamic problems, low self-starting torque, low efficiencies at low tip-speed ratios, and complicated unsteady flow behavior, due to dynamic

## Darrieus Wind Turbine Rotors
A Darrieus Wind Turbine Rotor is a type of vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) invented
by Georges Jean Marie Darrieus in 1931. It is widely recognized for its distinctive eggbeater shaped or H-shaped rotor design as shown in Fig. 3. This turbine comprise of
various aerofoil blades fixed on rotating framework or shaft. Blade’s curvature permits
blades to get stressed only with tension at maximum rotational speeds. Additionally,
WTT (Wind Tunnel Tests) finds applicability in assessing the performance of small

WTT undertaken on a DWT. Thus, a single VAWT has been mounted on three tunnels with various test sizes. Angular velocity, torque, power coefficient, TSR (Tip Speed

slot parameters such as inclination angle, dimensions, slot location and parameters have
been optimised for the NACA airfoil. This is generally utilised in the Darrieus turbines.
ANSYS-FLUENT code has been employed to model flow over turbine. Results explore
that VAWT with SA (Slotted Airfoil) possess minimum TSR in comparison to BL (Baseline) turbine which is related with the capability to generate maximum torque at minimum rotational speeds. Analysing aerodynamic performance of SA explores the delay

motion of turbine kind has been undertaken involving tip losses, wake/blade interaction and vortex propagation. Results exhibited that 3D flow effects impacting blades of
Darrieus rotor have been significantly complex in comparison to the assumption by low
fidelity models. It has been frequently utilised for designing that robustly vary during
revolution of rotor [10].
Typically, a wind turbine is a non-linear and higher order system which gets easily
impacted by disturbances [11]. Wind turbines (WTs) have to operate under stochastic
and high vibration wind loads and therefore require sophisticated load control methods. Aerodynamically, performance is highly dominated by the power coefficient (Cp),
tip speed ratio (λ), and dynamic stall behavior, and optimum Cp is usually attained in an
ideal λ in steady inflow conditions. State Space Control (SSC) and Disturbance Accommodating Control (DAC) are very useful in controlling coupled structural-aerodynamic
dynamics and wind disturbance without compromising aerodynamic power capture [12,
13]. Model-based Fuzzy Logic (FL) controllers Two-mass model-based Fuzzy Logic (FL)
controllers allow sensorless control and exhibit better transient response and less oscillations than traditional PI controllers [14]. The power optimization, Maximum Power
Point Tracking (MPPT) with Tip Speed Ratio (TSR) control, which includes an outer
TSR loop with an inner rotor speed loop, operates around the best aerodynamic region
and has been shown to be better than the traditional Optimal Torque (OT) approaches
[15, 16]. Nonlinearity in aerodynamics associated with increased dynamic stall, wakes
interaction and turbulent inflow increases fault vulnerability under extreme conditions [17]. This is alleviated using Fault Tolerant Control (FTC) and Fault Detection

and Isolation (FDI) measures [18, 19]. LPV models with Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI)
methods can be applied to detect an error in faults and reconfigure a controller to maintain stability during pitch and torque faults and maintain aerodynamic efficiency [20].
## Types of DWT Rotors
Darrieus turbine possess minimum initiating torque and is highly efficient. These are
VAWTs having low torque and high velocity. It typically comprise of two to three blades.
Lift force generated on blade assembly having cross sectional airfoil produces power in
DWT. H-rotor turbine is the general kind of Darrieus VAWT which has recently gained
more popularity. It is highly suitable when wind speed is low than conventional designs
as a result of simple operation, construction and flexibility for facing wind from random direction. A new kind of Darrieus that possess effective starting capability under
minimum power coefficient loss has been determined by suggesting a Darrieus turbine having opening. It utilised an airfoil having opening and a typical airfoil situated
at trailing end of airfoil. This opening might enhance the torque coefficients during low
wind speed. It has also explored that this suggested method might resolve drawbacks
of Darrieus turbine (having minimum starting torque) and savonius Darrieus (having
more power coefficient loss) [21]. Findings reveal that desirable rotors of inner-opening include rotors with 0.60 and 0.48 as opening ratios. Moreover, rotors having 0.84
and 0.72 as outer ratios seem to be reliable devices and could be employed for replacing the rotor of savonius Darrieus turbine that consider loss of power coefficient, torque
coefficient etc. [22, 23]. Various method for describing self-initiation of H-blade rotor
has been explored. BEM (Blade Element Momentum) has been compared with 2D and
3D-CFD simulations with respect to TSR, power coefficient and trust force evolution
over blade revolution exhibited the strengths and limitations of the suggested method.
Start-up ability of H-Darrieus seems to be impacted by several factors like 3D aerodynamic impacts, blade’s FAR (Finite Aspect Ratio) and secondary flows. Outcomes
revealed the efficiency of endorsed system [24]. Another kind of DWT is J-shaped turbine. Simulation has been undertaken where the J-shaped profile has been utilised as
blade airfoil. It has been designed through elimination of pressure side fraction of Du06-
W-200 airfoil. Analysis has been undertaken to determine the enhancement of VAWT
initiating torque through J-shaped profile. In addition, power-curves for J-shaped and
traditional profiles have been computed and torque variation has been attained at various azimuth angles. Pressure field and vorticity around WT has also been explored.
Results represented the optimised performance of turbine for the considered profile that
removes the airfoil’s pressure side from high thickness towards edge of trailing. Besides,
through this profile, performance of WT gets intensified and self-initiation of turbine
and its TSR has been enhanced [25–27].
Similarly, J-shaped VAWT with rotors of three blade has been analysed. Correct comparisons have been suggested for three varied turbines having various sections of airfoil
namely NACAS 1046, NACA 0015 and NACA 0021. This comprised of general airfoil
design and J-shape of similar airfoils from two perceptions of WT with respect to noise
generation and performance. Complete analysis of performance of suggested design
and aero-acoustics have been undertaken through CFD. Two equations namely FW-H
(Ffowcs Williams and Hawkings) and URANS (Unsteady Reynolds Averaged Navier
Stokes) have been solved concurrently. Impacts of speed ratio, turbine solidity and cut

ratio have been analysed. For all the configurations, outcomes represented that J-shape
minimised the performance of wind turbine as it did not minimise noise of turbine. In
accordance with the results, J-shape design does not suit well for DWT with respect to
reduction of noise generation and turbine performance. Ghafoorian F et al. [28] investigates improving the aerodynamic performance and self-starting capability of hybrid
Darrieus-Savonius vertical axis wind turbines (VAWTs) by installing a curtain. CFD simulations were used to determine optimal curtain installation angles, specifically for the
upper and lower walls. The findings indicate that the curtain significantly enhances self-
starting capability and power coefficient, particularly at low tip speed ratios (TSRs), with
optimal upper and lower wall angles of 20° and 45° respectively. This configuration led to
a 35% increase in rotor efficiency compared to the solo hybrid rotor.
In addition, a Savonius kind of VAWT has been studied that presents simple construction in comparison to supplementary kinds of VAWTs. A comparative analysis has
been carried out for comparing helical turbine to confirm its performance coefficients
and aerodynamic characteristics with two stage turbine having similar characteristics.
Hence, the main merits of altered kind of helical savonius turbine could be explored and
compared with traditional turbine like two stage turbines. Information of trust oriented
parameters for this kind of method could afford valuable tool for analysing VAWT functioning in real-time [29]. Likewise, φ-shaped DWTs possess high potential claims due
to structural merits and Omni-directionality. Yet, the design for these turbines requires
an attentive optimisation to accomplish high aerodynamic performance by fitting the
wind variations. Optimising shape of this DWT with given inlet-wind speed range has
been undertaken. By including HSA (Heuristic Search Algorithm), CMAES (Covariance
Matrix Adaptation Evolutionary Strategy) into DMST (Double Multiple Streamtube
Model), three geometric rotor variables like blade number, proportion of radius above
half of height and equatorial radius are altered in accordance with fitness function which
was specifically constructed for satisfying the range requirements of inlet wind. Outcomes explore that this could show an optimised model when range of wind speed is
12.5% enhanced at optimal velocity compared to baseline [30].
## H-Type Darrieus Rotor Aerodynamics
H-rotor system possesses vertical and straight blades rather than standard shapes. It
has curved Darrieus turbine blades. Aerodynamic-simulation is difficult for this turbine
quantitatively and qualitatively due to dynamic stall and separation of flow throughout
the blades. The probable way of enhancing the power output of VAWTs is to analyse
its aerodynamic performance without and with winglets. Thus, simulation has been
undertaken through ANSYS-CFX software relying on incompressible RANS (Reynolds
Averaged Navier Stokes) equation and SM (Structured Meshing) technology. Empirical
outcomes and CFD of rotor’s power-coefficients have better agreement during range
of computations. Power coefficient of this VAWT with winglets have been found to
be 10%-19% that is higher than actual H-type VAWT. In individual revolution, output
torque corresponding to this turbine has also been larger than actual turbine at different azimuth angle. Torque increases to a maximum of 12.8%, negative torque gets
avoided as the TSR (Tip Speed Ratio) attains 1.53. Moreover, the maximum and minimum thrust enhances by 3.4% and 26.7%. In addition, vorticity distribution at tips of the
turbine blades have been weaker in comparison to H-rotor having no winglets. It has

been found that aerodynamic performance enhances near the area of turbine blade’s tip.
Winglets enhance the complete generation of torque, however, it does not enhance at
individual azimuth angle for individual blade. Outcomes might afford useful comparison for optimisation and design of further H-rotor VAWTs having winglet [31]. Further,
the start-up characteristics of H-type VAWT has been studied by analysing the impacts
of physical properties like blade counts and inertia on its power performance through
a start-up model. The huge blade counts permits in attaining high power coefficients
at TSR less than one that enhances the turbine’s self-starting ability [32]. Similarly, a
numerical methodology has been suggested to research self-starting characteristics of
DWT by considering the moment of inertia of turbine. Simulation initiates from stationary state. It persists until the last steady and periodic condition. In each of time-step,
IRV (Instantaneous Rotational Velocity) of turbine has been calculated in accordance
with second law of Newton. This has been based on the mechanical and instantaneous
aerodynamic forces acting on a turbine. Outcomes represented that, more time has
been needed by the turbine to attain its velocity as the inertia of rotor enhances. Turbine might also fail to attain the overall condition which halts the rotation. On contrary,
the turbine’s oscillation amplitude enhances with decrease in rotor inertia. This could
permit turbine for passing high resistant torques in comparison to conventional technique [33, 34]. Besides, H-kind of Darrieus VAWT possess the ability to operate when
wind speed is low, however it possess limited aerodynamic performance in comparison
to horizontal turbine. Accordingly, effective blade airfoil and wind less configuration has
been determined to improve H-rotor’s aerodynamic performance. Wind lens has been
included in straight bladed turbine. Following this, the impacts of power extension has
been analysed through computational techniques. Various configurations of wind lens
have been employed to HAWTs. This similarity has also been efficient for H-rotor DWT.
It has been found that comprehensive DWT performance could be highly improved
through optimisation methods. Eight new airfoil series appropriate for H-rotor DWT
have been researched for effective aerodynamic performance. RISφ-A1-24, NACA0015,
S-1046, FX-L-142 K, DU06-W-200, NACA 63–418, AF300, LS (1)-0417 and FX-L-142 K
have been suitable airfoils for individual series of H-rotor DWT. It has been summarised
that better performance have been accomplished by symmetric airfoils in comparison to
non-symmetric airfoils [35, 36].
## Evolution of H-Type DWT
Early VAWT have been built based on its aerodynamic drag, while, reverse drag has
been removed by encompassing advancing blade for producing net torque. It has also
been comprehended that lift force has been efficient parameter than drag. Shape of blade
has to be varying on bottom and top to accomplish sufficient lift. Lift based DWT has
been introduced by an aeronautical engineer of French named Darrieus in 1925. Though
small models were built by Darrieus for establishing his creation, generation model having 7 kW power was constructed by Morel in 1950. Generally, VAWT could be partitioned into savonius rotor, Darrieus rotor, DWT, integrated Darrieus and savonius rotor,
sistan kind of wind mill, zephyr turbine and 2-leaf semi-rotary VAWT. In 1931, DWT
has been initially designed. Among VAWTs, DWTs explore high efficiency values. As
study on VAWT attained momentum during initial 1980s, various configurations were
considered and tested. Initially, various failures have been encountered and it was also

restricted to funding in comparison to HAWT. Several innovative ideas have also been
not commercialised or unexplored. Over the past thirty years, comprehensive study on
the Darrieus rotor resulted in expansion of MST (Megawatt Scale Turbines). Various
issues have been solved that was earlier limiting the ability of these turbines during its
initial development. Several innovative ideas on the Darrieus rotor strengthen the ability for effective VAWT compared to HAWT. Persisting study on the Darrieus rotor like
AHDT has been promising and vital for transformation as device of low wind speed. The
limitations of HAWT gets unfold when the size enhances. This leads to transformation
for concentrating on DWT rotor as the substitute. On contrary, H-type DWT, a kind of
VAWT possess several advantages. It has simplified structure, simple structure and light
weight. It also explores high power coefficient of wind and extensive application views
[37]. Hence, the VAWT wakes have been researched numerically and empirically with
several fidelity degrees like TSR, solidity ratio and Reynolds number. However, numerical and empirical assessment of impacts associated with foil shape in the hydrokinetic
turbine wake has been unstudied to find ideal distance amongst two turbines which
afford ideal performance. Various simulations have been undertaken based on 2D-3D
incompressible VFM (Viscous Flow Model). Effect of foil shape has been analysed with
various camber and thickness ratio of similar hydrofoil series of a three bladed H-type
DWT. The degree to which it effects the wake structure and hydrodynamic performance
of turbine has been assessed. At TSR having blade configuration with high power coefficient, non-symmetrical profile’s wake recovery has been effective than symmetrical
profile. NACA 4418 and NACA 2418 waves have been similar. But power coefficient
corresponding to NACA 4418 has been 4.5% higher when compared to NACA 2418.
Thus, outcomes attained from 2D CFD simulations afford valuable and faster data for
comprehending their process. Results of this research will also afford few guidelines for
enhancing the efficiency of turbine and will support the installation design of turbine
farm. NACA 4418 profile has been found to be suitable for application on hydro-farm in
a channel flow [38].

rotation have been solved. Energy coefficient and flow field have been analysed for varied pitch angle and solidity of turbine. Outcomes revealed that pitch angle and solidity
impact VAWT’s aerodynamic performance to high extent [39]. In addition, the starting behaviour of H-DWT have been analysed. Basic starting have also been researched.
Parameters of rotor geometry have also been researched that regulate the unsteady

improved and innovative VAWT designs. Accordingly, the impacts of the blade attachments on asymmetric H-Darrieus turbine has been analysed when wind speed is low.

top. Thus, it could be summarised that turbine blade having high thickness degree at
top of portion will enhance the turbine’s performance [55]. Similarly, performance of
VAWT having straight blades have been analysed with respect to power. Field test and
WTT (Wind Tunnel Test) has been used for analysis. Test rotor having two blades and
NACA0021 profile has been considered. In the field test, wake velocity permits fast
recovery compared to wake-velocity in WTT. Moreover, wake velocity reduced with
enhancement of TSR [56]. Various traditional works considered different blade profile

## The Reliability of Two-Dimensional Versus Three-Dimensional Simulations
In CFD, dimensionality significantly affects prediction reliability. Two-dimensional simulations neglect tip losses and spanwise flow, often leading to Cp overprediction and
inaccurate wake vortex representation. Comparative 2D–3D studies reveal notable discrepancies in Cp, max and wake topology, particularly at moderate to high TSR values.
While well-calibrated 2D URANS models can reproduce general performance trends
and are suitable for early-stage design and comparative analysis, three-dimensional
simulations are essential for accurate prediction of absolute performance, blade loading, wake interaction, and farm-level assessments [96–98]. The reliability comparison

## Limitations
In the case of H-Darrieus CFD simulations, model validation is crucial since majority
of the RANS commonly used models have significant and well documented limitations,
especially when dynamic stall, vortex shedding and wake evolution is to be predicted
[80]. Validation is usually done by direct comparison of projected Cp-TSR curves and
torque coefficients with wind tunnel or tow tank data. The computational setup is usually tuned to allow errors in prediction of power coefficients to be kept to a level of less
than 5–10% when the computational setup is set up carefully and that is with proper
domain size, mesh refinements, time-step dependence and parasitic torques corrections.
Nevertheless, according to critical reviews, it is inappropriate to associate model reliability only with matching the power coefficient. It should also be accurately validated by
comparing the structures of wake vortices, pressure fields, dynamic stall signatures and
vorticity fields, particularly in comparing the differences between two-dimensional and
three-dimensional models or in choosing between different turbulence closures. Also,
some literature confirms complete CFD techniques instead of using isolated simulation
cases. This wider validation methodology usually entails grid and time sensitivity analysis in a systematic way, comparison against various experimental data and cross-compensation with more faithful Navier-Stokes models or actuator-line models [99, 100].
## Numerical Uncertainties
The extensive validation steps are useful in determining numerical uncertainties and
biases in the turbulence models, and they boost performance prediction trust. However,
pitfalls still exist: excessive fine-tuning of models simulates one experimental Cp vs. TSR
curve to fit a particular experimental Cp vs. TSR curve, and when the same model is
used with a different geometry or operating point, gives false results. Moreover, uncertainty in numerical results for H-Darrieus CFD arises from discretization, turbulence
modeling, boundary conditions, geometric simplifications, and experimental reference
data. Reliable simulations require systematic grid and time-step convergence studies,
turbulence model comparison, sensitivity analysis, and comprehensive validation against
multiple experimental metrics rather than reliance on a single performance parameter
[101–104]. Large comparative studies demonstrate that simulation results vary significantly depending on the turbulence model selected [102]. Models such as Spalart–Allmaras (SA), RNG k–ε, realizable k–ε, and k–ω often fail to accurately reproduce VAWT
power curves and blade load distributions across different operating regimes, leading to
large errors in Cp and stall onset prediction [93]. Among two-equation models, the fully
turbulent k–ω SST formulation is generally considered the most robust; however, it still
tends to delay flow separation and under-resolve dynamic stall phenomena, often resulting in Cp overprediction near peak TSR conditions. Fundamentally, RANS models rely
on an isotropic eddy-viscosity assumption, which is poorly suited for the strongly separated, rotating, and highly anisotropic flow structures present in H-Darrieus wakes and
during deep stall events [105, 106]. There is transition and hybrid RANS in the case of
transition and hybrid RANS. The boundaries of laminar separation and stall processes
are more accurately represented by Transition SST-based formulations and lead to lower
Cp overprediction, especially at low Reynolds numbers. But two-dimensional URANS is
not able to model inherently three-dimensional observations like tip vortices and spanwise flow. Hybrid DES/DDES methods enhance the resolution of unsteady vortices at a

very high cost of computation and refined the near-wall grids, which is not very practical
[105, 106].

## Applications and Trends
VAWT have been utilised in rural and urban areas for commercial, industrial and residential applications. Installation of WTs in cities could assist in achieving a smooth
energy demand. Recently, it is utilised for feeding commercial grid. In accordance with
the resources of urban wind, the power production distribution has turned to be the
significant issue. It could satisfy the requests for consumption in rural areas thereby
subsidise to smart grid. VAWT and HAWT has been accepted in recent times. Huge
number of WTs have been installed globally in last few decades. For instance: India and
China have developed half of overall turbine installation throughout the world. All these
corresponds to production in large-scale. As stated by WWEA (World Wind Energy
Association) record, installation of WTs increased by 5% in comparison to early years.
The estimated capacity of this turbine was higher than 945 MW that enhanced 14% in
comparison to previous year. As VAWTs have been trustworthy for regionalised energy
production, various system integrators and manufacturers have found significance in
investment in this field. VAWT components and suppliers include urban-green energy,
Hi-VAWT, Global-wing group, VBINE energy, clean-field energy etc. [91]. Similarly,
installing H-type VAWT has limited cases by intrinsic start-up challenges related with
this kind of turbine which could be vital in environments having minimum wind speed.
H-type DWT has turned to be trending. Accordingly, CFD modelling has been afforded
for analysing the start-up characteristics related with this kind of turbines. ANSYS Fluent has been employed for analysis. In contradiction to traditional work, turbine has
been left free for acceleration in accordance with experience of torque over time. Comprehensive analysis has been accomplished considering grid generation and turbulence
modelling that have been key to conform accuracy for this analysis [92]. Outcomes from
simulation revealed better agreement based on empirical data. Techniques provide high
accuracy level confirming its valuability for identical issues. Similarly, the CFD setup has
been employed for assessing the impacts of various geometric features of turbine rotor
on the initiating features. Cambered and symmetric airfoils having varied thickness with
different pitch angles have been analysed. Ideal start-up features have been perceived
with usage of NACA2418 showing 1.5 as pitch angle. This configuration minimised the
start-up period while maintaining the peak performance of the turbine [57].
## Research Gaps
Various gaps identified through analysis of the existing works are listed below.

•
One of the major limitations of H-type Darrieus turbines is poor self-starting
capability. Although several passive and active enhancement methods (e.g., variable
pitch, helical blades, starter motors) have been proposed, comprehensive parametric
studies quantifying their effects under different wind conditions are still insufficient.
•
Experimental data, especially for dynamic stall, wake development, and self-starting
behaviour of H-rotors are limited. Most field or wind tunnel tests focus on averaged
quantities, neglecting transient behaviours. Furthermore, comparative validation of
CFD simulations against time-resolved experimental results (e.g., via Particle Image
Velocimetry) is lacking.
•
Although several blade profiles (e.g., NACA 0018, NACA 0021, S-series) have been
used, a systematic comparison of airfoils under dynamic stall and low TSRs is still
lacking. Additionally, the influence of profile geometry on hysteresis behaviour and
lift-drag characteristics in deep stall regimes requires further analysis.
•
Most aerodynamic studies do not consider the impact of flow-induced vibrations
and aeroelastic coupling in the rotor arms and blades. These factors can significantly
affect fatigue life and operational stability, particularly at higher wind speeds or in
off-design conditions.
•
Research on hybrid H-rotor systems, such as those combining Savonius-Darrieus or
dual-rotor H-type configurations, is limited. These systems show promise for self-
starting and improved low-speed performance but lack detailed aerodynamic and
structural investigations.
•
While many CFD and performance evaluations are performed at optimal or rated
wind speeds, the behaviour of H-type turbines under urban wind profiles, including
turbulent and sheared inflows, is not well understood. Research into low Reynolds
number aerodynamics, noise sensitivity, and turbine siting in built environments is
limited.
•
There is a lack of systematic studies employing multi-objective optimization
techniques to design optimal blade shapes, aspect ratios, solidity, and strut
configurations for minimizing drag while maximizing lift. Trade-offs between
efficiency, noise emission, and structural integrity have rarely been investigated in a
unified optimization framework.
•
A majority of simulations still rely on two-dimensional CFD models, which are
insufficient to capture complex flow features like blade tip vortices, arm-induced
disturbances, and vertical wake behaviour in H-type Darrieus turbines. Full 3D CFD
and Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) simulations are essential to account for the
influence of structural flexibility and unsteady aerodynamics on performance.
•
The application of bio-inspired blade geometries, flexible or morphing structures,
and variable camber blades in H-type Darrieus turbines is an underexplored area.
These concepts could enhance performance across a wider range of wind conditions
and reduce fatigue loads.
•
Most simulation models omit key resistive torque components such as generator
load, shaft damping, and bearing friction, leading to discrepancies in angular velocity
predictions. The lack of empirical data hampers the quantification of torque behavior
during the startup phase. Furthermore, most studies utilize theoretical angle-of-
attack values, whereas accurate modeling requires the effective angle-of-attack,
especially under dynamic stall conditions.

•
Noise generation mechanisms, wake–vortex–acoustic coupling, and mitigation
strategies are largely unexplored, despite their importance for urban applications.
Moreover, most validations are based on wind tunnel data; real-world field
measurements under natural inflow conditions are scarce, leading to uncertainty in
CFD-to-reality performance gaps.
## Conclusion
The research aimed to analyse the aerodynamic performance of H-type DWT. Various
factors influenced the efficacy of this turbine which was explored through this analysis
namely ITV, blade thickness, pitch angle etc. Various designs have been considered by
the traditional studies. Different simulations have been undertaken by them. This study
tabulated the important design considerations and CFD simulations of conventional
works encompassing the blade profiles such as NACA 0021, NACA4415, DU06W200,
NACA0020, NACA0018, NACA0022, NACA634-221, NACA0015 with 2D and 3D CFD
simulations. A critical analysis was undertaken by considering different parameters such
as power coefficients, wind speed, blade thickness, drag force TSR, torque coefficient,
pitch angle, blade number, blade profile, moment of inertia, turbulence intensity, vorticity, azimuthal angle etc. Analysis revealed that TSR, wind speed, drag force, blade thickness and torque coefficient was found to be highly considered at a rate of 68%. Whereas,
vibration, dimple effect, Chord to diameter ratio, noise generation and blade aspect ratio
was least considered. With respect to application, H-type DWT has various residential, economical and commercial benefits. However, it has various demerits which are
also presented in this study. To solves these issues, this study suggested to undertake 3D
simulations and consider significant parameters. It has also afforded other suggestions
which can be considered by researchers for effective employment of this turbine in various applications.
