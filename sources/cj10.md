# Validation Case: NACA 0012 Airfoil at Mach 0.15

This validation case aims to assess the accuracy of the simulation results for the classical `NACA 0012` airfoil operating at Mach `0.15`, by comparing them with experimental and reference data provided in the extended NASA Technical Report `[NAS-2016-01]`.

The NACA 0012 is a widely studied symmetric airfoil, with extensive experimental data available, particularly from the work of Charles Ladson in the report *Effects of Independent Variation of Mach and Reynolds Numbers on the Low-Speed Aerodynamic Characteristics of the NACA 0012 Airfoil Section.* By comparing numerical predictions of lift, drag, and pressure distribution with the experimental results, this case serves to evaluate the robustness and accuracy of the turbulence model and overall simulation setup.

## Geometry

The NACA 0012 is a symmetric, 4-digit airfoil characterized by a maximum thickness of `12%` of the chord, with no camber. In this case, the chord length is set to approximately `1.0 m`.

The trailing edge of the airfoil was simplified by adding a small chamfer to improve boundary layer meshing and avoid the formation of stair-step elements near the sharp edge.

## Analysis Type and Mesh

- Tool type: `OpenFOAM`
- Analysis type: turbulent incompressible fluid flow

The mesh was generated using the Standard mesh algorithm with Extrusion mesh refinement to extrude the mesh in the spanwise direction with a single cell, resulting in a pseudo-2D domain. This setup maintains only one element across the domain thickness. The computational domain measures `60 m x 40 m`.

Three mesh densities were tested at `AoA = 10 deg` and compared against the reference values `CL ≈ 1.066` and `CD ≈ 0.012`:

| Mesh | Grid Size | CL | CD | CL Error % | CD Error % |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Coarse | 103434 | 1.064 | 0.013 | 0.2 | 7.4 |
| Moderate | 218499 | 1.074 | 0.012 | 0.5 | 0.0 |
| Fine | 444101 | 1.074 | 0.012 | 0.7 | 0.0 |

The moderate mesh was selected because of its accuracy-to-simulation-time ratio.

The mesh resolution ensures `y+ ~ 1` for all configurations, allowing accurate resolution of the near-wall region.

Wall treatment:

- Full resolution in the near-wall region: the first cell lies at most at the boundary of the laminar sub-layer. Here, `y+` is `1` or below.
- Use of wall functions in the near-wall region: typically `30 <= y+ <= 300`.

An average `y+` value of `1` was used for the inflation layer around the airfoil. The `k-omega SST` turbulence model was chosen, with full-resolution near-wall treatment.

## Simulation Setup

Air with a kinematic viscosity of `8.6 x 10^-6 m^2/s` was assigned to the fluid domain. The viscosity was assumed with respect to the Reynolds number of `6,000,000`.

Boundary conditions:

| Parameter | Inlet | Airfoil Faces | Lateral Faces | Outlet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Velocity | `52.08 m/s` | Full Resolution | `Empty2D` | Zero Gradient |
| Turb. kinetic energy `k` | `1.627 m^2/s^2` | Full Resolution | `Empty2D` | Zero Gradient |
| Specific dissipation rate `omega` | `128 1/s` | Full Resolution | `Empty2D` | Zero Gradient |
| Pressure | Zero Gradient | Full Resolution | `Empty2D` | `0` |

The free-stream velocity is `52.08 m/s`, and the angle of attack is imposed through the velocity components as `52.08 * (cos(alpha), sin(alpha))` for each simulated angle `0`, `2.5`, `5`, `10`, and `15` degrees.

To improve convergence and stability, the interpolation schemes for the velocity and pressure-gradient schemes were changed from `Least Squares` to `Gauss-Linear`.

## Result Comparison

The case uses SimScale's built-in result control for force and moment coefficients, allowing user-defined lift direction, drag direction, reference length, and reference area.

The reported comparison shows good agreement with experimental data for lift coefficient, drag coefficient, and pressure coefficient on the airfoil surface.

## Results

The case presents a velocity contour and pressure-coefficient contour for `AoA = 15 deg`, showing boundary-layer development near the wall and the corresponding pressure loading on the airfoil.
