# A Simulation Study on Airfoils Using VAWT Design for Low Wind Speed Application

P. D Abd. Aziz, A. K. R Mohamad, F.Z Hamidon, N. Mohamad, N. Salleh, N. Mohd Yunus

Electrical Technology Section, Universiti Kuala Lumpur-British Malaysian Institute, Batu 8, Jalan Sungai Pusu, 53100 Gombak, Selangor, Malaysia

## Abstract

This paper presents the simulation study on the airfoil based on the vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT) for low wind speed application ranging 2 to 8 meter per seconds. The main advantage VAWT compared to the horizontal axis wind turbine (HAWT) is the ability to capture wind from any direction. Therefore, the yaw system is not required to turn the rotor towards the wind direction. Furthermore, the noise level produced during the rotation making it suitable to be installed on residential and urban environment. However, the major drawbacks of VAWT over HAWT are less efficient due to drag and turbulent force that the blade produces and low starting torque. The aim of this study is to develop a VAWT design equipped with the advantages mentioned and to overcome the VAWT drawbacks. The National Advisory Committee of Aeronautics (NACA) for both airfoil series 0012 and 0015 were analyzed using simulation provided in the Qblade software. The graphical relation between optimal angles of attack (AoA) and the Reynolds number which predicts the flow patterns of the wind is analyzed. Based on this, the AoA that generate high lift over drag ratio (Cl/Cd) can be identified. The ratio of Cl/Cd for the airfoils; NACA 0012 and NACA 0015 are used to analyze the relationship between Tip Speed Ratio (TSR) and the Power Coefficient (Cp) performance. The number of blades is also tested to justify the effect on the power produced in low wind speed condition. Comparison of the simulated airfoils is presented in this paper. A simulated design of small scale size VAWT is made to test the effect of the blades number and their respective Cp and TSR versus wind speed. With the proper airfoils design that suits the low wind speed condition, the turbine can functions efficiently as well as maximize the power produced by turbine.

Keywords: VAWT, HAWT, NACA series, AoA, Reynolds number, TSR, Cl/Cd ratio and Cp

## I. Introduction

In a few decades, the non-renewable energy sources such as coal, gas, and oil will be limited and the production will be minimized [1]. Due to high in demand of these sources, there will be an increment in price and gives great effect to well develop countries. Non-renewable energy has some global climate change issue and carbon dioxide as well as sulphur dioxide emission from its production [2]. The renewable energy generation is the solution that is being implemented to replace non-renewable energy as primary sources of power generation [2]. Renewable energy is cost effective, low to non-carbon emission, climate friendly and unlimited. Some forms of renewable energy are solar, tidal, biomass and bio-fuel, geothermal, and wind. Wind turbines are widely used all over the world and it is regarded as an alternative source for electrical power generation [3]. Wind turbine energy return of energy invested (EROEI) and can be considered high among the renewable energy generation with value of 8, compared to solar power which is 5 [3].

Wind turbine can be classified into two types; horizontal axis wind turbine (HAWT) and vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT). VAWT is the new discovery and has the ability to capture wind from any directions. Savonius is the VAWT type and incorporated the drag force. Small wind turbines are generally those rated at 10kW or less. Wind turbines system consists of a turbine, a tower, a controller, a grid connected inverter and a meter. Malaysia falls on the region in Southeast Asia which has a very low wind speed which is below 5m/s [4].

Problem of wind turbine is associated with the designing of the wind turbine itself, some of the wind turbine are designed is too big to harness more energy from the wind. This is true if the wind speed is high but for country with a low wind speed, designing a small wind turbine is more efficient in harnessing the wind [5, 6]. Some of the researchers have started to harness the power from wind by designing a small wind turbine which can operate at low wind speed with low Reynolds number [7, 9]. However, it might be impossible to even make the rotor spin and harness great energy, due to the low wind speed.

## II. Airfoil and Wind Turbine Theory

In a wind turbine design, airfoil configuration for blades is one of the most important factors for high efficiency of the turbine to convert kinetic energy from wind into mechanical energy using rotor and generator that converts mechanical energy to electrical energy. The cut in speed is when the rotor starts to generate power, while the cut out speed is when the rotor start to reduce its speeds and power generated [8, 9]. The details are as shown in Figure 1.

The 'Lift coefficient' (Cl) is the lifting force generated by the airfoil and 'Drag coefficient' (Cd) is the drag or resistance force generated in the airfoil. These two forces are related to each other and important in giving precise information on the lift to drag ratio (Cl/Cd) in the airfoil configuration. Both forces are commonly associated with the 'Angle of Attack' (AoA) to determine the forces to be high or low at a particular AoA. The maximum Cl for a certain AoA for an airfoil is called stall angle. The common stall angle for airfoils is between 10 to 15 degrees [10].

The solidity of the rotor also affects the performance of the turbine, using the solidity equation in 1, the solidity value can be determine for the numbers of blades selected. For the three, five and eight blades, the solidity values are 0.51, 0.85 and 1.36 respectively. Based on this, the higher number of blades gives high solidity value which causes higher drag force. Three blades give an optimal solidity which can produce more power due to high performance of Cp.

The torque of the rotor against different wind speed was also investigated for the number of blades used. It was found that the higher the number of blades results in better torque in low wind speed condition, torque is important for the turbine to be self-rotating and work as stand alone application. The drawback of high number of blades is their efficiency to produce power. Low number of blades has high efficiency and power coefficient but due to inability of low number of blades to self rotate, it is advisable to hybrid it with drag type rotor to overcome the self-starting problem.

The higher number of blades produces more drag force than the lower number of blades. Furthermore, the value of TSR is low but high in torque in low wind speed condition which means it is self-operated at low wind speed condition. But it has low power efficiency compared to lower number of blades. If the TSR is low, it is hard to match with the generator which operates at low TSR.

## V. Conclusion

The conclusion that can be made from the simulation study is:

- NACA0015 show a better performance under a high wind speed compared to the NACA0012. The optimal stall angle is around 10 to 15 degrees for both airfoils. The power output produce for NACA 0015 is higher than NACA 0012 under higher wind speed.
- The number of blades affects the TSR and Cp produced by the wind turbine. Three blades are optimal and have stable power coefficient than five and eight. Higher number of blades employed more drag forces and torque, making it self starting and efficient under low wind speed condition.
