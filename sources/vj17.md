## Abstract

In this research study, the optimization study of an airfoil type Savonius turbine is presented, focusing on maximizing the power coefficient. For this purpose, a Discrete Vortex Method (DVM) code for calculating power coefficient, a Class Shape Transfer (CST) function code for generating coordinates of airfoils and a Salp Swarm Algorithm (SSA) code for optimization have been developed. These three codes are coupled and an optimization procedure is generated. The decision variables of the mentioned problem are the CST function coefficients and the objective is the power coefficient. For verification of the results, CFD simulations are utilized. The results indicate that the achieved geometry could increase the power coefficient of a conventional Savonius turbine up to 27%. Also, the physical results indicate that the suction area and pressure magnitude of the pressure side of the airfoil type turbine is more extensive than simple type Savonius turbine at the optimization TSR. Therefore, in the airfoil type wind turbine, the great pressure difference causes more force at the surface of blades and in results, an increase in power coefficient occurs.

## 1. Introduction

In recent years, for many reasons, including fossil fuels reduction, environmental pollution, and global warming, energy production from renewable sources is a priority of the international community. Among different renewable energy types, which include solar, wind, hydro, and so forth, wind energy has been studied for reasons such as being clear and abundant. Primarily, there are two categories of wind turbines: the horizontal axis and the vertical axis wind turbines. Different types of vertical axis wind turbines exist, but the two major groups are Savonius (drag-type) and Darrieus (lift-type) turbines.

Generally, vertical axis wind turbines have advantages over horizontal axis wind turbines because of their small tower sway, their independence to wind direction, their less noise production, being less obstruction for birds, maintenance costs, etc. Both Savonius and Darrieus turbines have their own advantages and disadvantages. The advantage of Darrieus rotors is their higher efficiency in comparison to Savonius turbines, while the most significant advantage of Savonius turbines is self-starting ability at low wind speed.

The focus of the present research study is on the Savonius type of wind turbines. The paper uses DVM for the initial stage, CST for airfoil coordinate generation, SSA for optimization, and CFD for verification.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1. Discrete vortex method

The modeling of flow is carried out using the discrete vortex method, which is a subset of a singular-element-based numerical solution and the geometry has been discretized to panels by putting the vortex in the middle of each panel. The method assumes inviscid, irrotational, incompressible, unsteady flow with no body forces.

### 2.1.1. Discretization of the airfoil

The two same airfoils create the geometry of the Savonius wind turbine cross section. The rotor diameter and overlap ratio are the fundamental parameters in the Savonius rotor. According to Fig. 2, each airfoil is divided into n panels, then a bound vortex is distributed in the middle of the panels. Control points are placed at the beginning and the end of each panel, and free vortices are emitted from the leading edge and trailing edge.

![Source figure](vj17-fig2.jpg)
Original caption: Fig. 2. Discretization of the airfoils.

### 2.1.2. Unsteady flow model

The upcoming wind velocity is U0 and the rotor is rotating with angular speed u. The velocity is derived from the complex potential. The flow tangency condition is applied to the control points, and Kutta-Joukowski conditions plus Kelvin's theorem provide the remaining equations.

### 2.1.3. Numerical calculation

If the spacing between the two vortices is small, the velocity becomes unrealistically large. To avoid this, the calculation is modified. If the distance between vortices is smaller than s = 0.05(chord), the velocity is regularized. If the space between a vortex and the body is smaller than s, the vortex is removed for the effect of viscosity. Roll-up is used to model vortex shedding.

### 2.1.4. Torque and power coefficient

The pressure is computed near the panel with the Bernoulli equation, the pressure difference between upper and lower curves gives the aerodynamic force, and torque, power, and power coefficient are then computed from the resulting force and rotor speed.

### 2.1.5. Validation of developed code

The DVM results are compared against experimental data from Sheldahl et al. across a range of TSR values. The paper says the trend matches experiment and the error is acceptable for this method.

### 2.2. Class shape transfer (CST) function method

In airfoil optimization, if each point of pressure and suction surfaces was considered as a decision variable, the complexity of the optimization process would be increased. Therefore, CST is used to decrease that complexity. CST parameterization expresses the upper and lower surfaces of an airfoil as polynomial functions with class and shape terms.

### 2.3. Salp swarm algorithm (SSA)

SSA is a recently proposed nature-inspired optimization method. The population is divided into a leader and followers. The leader updates position with respect to the food source, and followers update according to Newton's law of motion.

### 2.4. The optimization procedure

The CST curvature parameters are considered as decision parameters and the power coefficient is considered as the objective function. An initial population is generated, its coordinates are calculated using CST, the DVM code evaluates power coefficient, and SSA generates new solutions until convergence.

### 3.2. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) verification

The achieved results are verified using CFD for both the semicircular Savonius turbine and the airfoil type turbine. The paper assumes the inner curve length of both turbines is equal so the viscous drag comparison is reasonable. The optimum airfoil improves the wind turbine performance by about 27%.

The utilized computational domain contains two sub-regions: a rotating sphere with diameter 1.2D and a fixed cube with dimensions W = 12D, L = 20D, H = 12D. The inlet boundary is velocity inlet, the outlet boundary is pressure outlet, and the walls are treated as wall boundaries.

## 4. Conclusion

The paper concludes that DVM, CST, and SSA can be coupled to optimize Savonius rotor geometry. The optimum airfoil-type Savonius rotor gives a 27% power-coefficient improvement over the semicircular baseline, and the authors recommend future work on 3D DVM, other curve parameterizations, different TSRs, CFD-based optimization, and experiments on the optimum airfoil.
