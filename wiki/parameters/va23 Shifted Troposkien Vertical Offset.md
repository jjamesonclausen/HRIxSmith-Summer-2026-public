## Shifted Troposkien Vertical Offset

This `va23` paper studies how far one troposkien blade should be shifted vertically relative to the other to reduce blade-wake interaction without giving away too much blade area or solidity. (source: sources/va23.md)

- The source compares three settings: no shift (conventional), `50%` shift, and `100%` shift. (source: sources/va23.md)
- All three cases keep the same swept area, maximum radius, total height, chord length, blade count, and airfoil so the changed quantity is mainly the shift-driven blade geometry. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The `50%` shifted case gives the best reported overall result. At `600 rpm`, its corrected peak is about `Cp = 0.23`, roughly `10%` above the conventional corrected peak near `Cp = 0.21`. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The same `50%` shifted case also reaches an uncorrected peak near `Cp = 0.286` at `700 rpm`, remaining the strongest performer among the three concepts. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The `100%` shifted case reduces blade-wake interaction further, but the paper says the shorter blade length and lower solidity reduce power enough that it underperforms the `50%` shift near the peak-power region. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The source therefore presents vertical shift as a non-monotonic tradeoff: some shift helps by reducing wake interference, but too much shift gives away too much blade length and solidity. (source: sources/va23.md)
