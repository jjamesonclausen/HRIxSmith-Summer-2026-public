## Blade Number

This `vj22` study varies blade number on the simulated low-wind-speed rotor to compare `Cp`, `TSR`, solidity, and torque behavior. (source: sources/vj22.md)

## Parameter

- The source compares `3`, `5`, and `8` blades. (source: sources/vj22.md)
- It reports calculated `TSRmax` values of `4.2`, `2.5`, and `1.6` for `3`, `5`, and `8` blades respectively. (source: sources/vj22.md)
- It also reports rotor solidity values of `0.51`, `0.85`, and `1.36` for `3`, `5`, and `8` blades respectively. (source: sources/vj22.md)

## Outcome

- The paper says `3` blades produce the highest and most stable power coefficient, with maximum `Cp` about `0.3` at `TSR` between `3` and `4`. (source: sources/vj22.md)
- It reports lower efficiency for `5` and `8` blades because the higher blade count raises solidity and drag. (source: sources/vj22.md)
- At the same time, the paper says higher blade count gives better torque in low wind speed conditions and makes self-rotation easier. (source: sources/vj22.md)
- The source therefore frames blade number as a direct tradeoff between efficiency and startup torque for this low-wind-speed design space. (source: sources/vj22.md)
