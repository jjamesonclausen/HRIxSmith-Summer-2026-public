## Helix Angle

This `va7` study changes the blade helix angle of a helical VAWT to investigate how it affects power output, wake structure, and load smoothness. (source: sources/va7.md)

## Parameter Change

- The study compares helix angles of 60 degrees, 90 degrees, and 120 degrees. (source: sources/va7.md)
- The rest of the baseline geometry is held constant: NACA0015 blades, no pitch, 210 mm chord, 1 m diameter, and 3 m height. (source: sources/va7.md)

## Outcome

- For a 60 degree helix angle, performance is maximum at TSR 3.3 and is better than the other helical and straight-bladed cases discussed in the paper. (source: sources/va7.md)
- The 90 degree case gives performance comparable with the straight-bladed turbine, but its peak shifts to a higher TSR than the straight-bladed case. (source: sources/va7.md)
- The 120 degree case has the weakest power-performance curve of the three helical cases, but it spreads power generation over a broader azimuth range and reduces cyclic variation. (source: sources/va7.md)
- Increasing helix angle reduces the standard deviation of `Cp`, so shaft ripple and cyclic loading become smoother as helix angle increases. (source: sources/va7.md)
- The source says the straight-bladed case has the highest standard deviation, about 85 percent higher than the 120 degree helical case in the comparison highlighted by the authors. (source: sources/va7.md)
- The parameter therefore gives a strong smoothing benefit, but with a tradeoff against peak power at larger helix angles. (source: sources/va7.md)
