## Blade Number

This `vj4` study compares two-bladed and three-bladed small VAWT architectures at fixed solidity and Reynolds number. (source: sources/vj4.md)

## Parameter Change

- The source compares two-bladed and three-bladed H-Darrieus variants within a matched aerodynamic study. (source: sources/vj4.md)
- The comparison is intended to isolate the effect of blade count rather than solidity changes. (source: sources/vj4.md)

## Outcome

- The source reports that the two-bladed rotor shows the smallest power coefficient compared with the three-bladed rotors in the comparison set. (source: sources/vj4.md)
- In the fixed-Reynolds-number comparison reported in Table 3, the two-bladed case gives `Q = 5.5 Nm` and `RQ = 1.47`, while the corresponding three-bladed case gives `Q = 13.2 Nm` and `RQ = 0.29`. (source: sources/vj4.md)
- The paper concludes that, excluding the increased-Reynolds-number special case, a three-bladed configuration is better than the corresponding two-bladed one. (source: sources/vj4.md)
- The source also warns that part of the apparent improvement in one comparison case comes from higher rotational speed and Reynolds number rather than blade count alone, so blade number is not the only driver. (source: sources/vj4.md)
- The tradeoff is practical rather than purely aerodynamic: three blades improve output and smoothness, but cost and construction complexity increase. (source: sources/vj4.md)
