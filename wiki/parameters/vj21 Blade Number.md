## Blade Number

This `vj21` paper compares one-, two-, and three-bladed straight-bladed VAWT layouts as a structural and performance tradeoff rather than a single-metric optimization. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Parameter

- A single blade is described as technically feasible but requiring a counterweight and lacking self-starting because forward torque is not produced at all blade positions. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The paper says two-bladed rotors have lower construction and assembly cost plus better strength-to-weight ratio. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- Three-bladed rotors are described as structurally non-directional and more favorable in response than two blades of the same chord length. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Outcome

- The source favors three blades when smoother running, lower torque ripple, and better structural dynamics matter more than lowest cost. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It also says a three-bladed rotor reduces cyclic fluctuation of torque and net force because the loads behave more like three-phase power instead of the more in-phase loading seen with two blades. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The paper still treats two blades as advantageous on cost and strength-to-weight grounds, so the net benefit is a tradeoff rather than a universal performance win. (source: sources/vj21.md)
