## Supporting Strut Shape

This `vj21` paper treats the profile of the supporting struts as an aerodynamic design variable because struts create parasitic drag that directly lowers net output. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Parameter

- The paper says a non-lifting airfoil with low zero-lift drag is preferable to rectangular, circular, or other bluffer strut profiles. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It cites the older `E862`, `E863`, and `E864` strut airfoils, then argues that they were not designed for the lower Reynolds numbers typical of smaller-capacity SB-VAWTs. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The source references a newer low-Re strut profile named `MI-STRUT1` for this application range. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Outcome

- The paper states that `MI-STRUT1` has lower zero-lift drag than `E862` across the tested Reynolds numbers, with the difference becoming larger below `RN 250,000`. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It also says `CP,net-lambda` curves with `MI-STRUT1` are better than with blunt-body or `E862` struts at `RN = 100,000` and `300,000`. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- Based on those findings, the source presents `MI-STRUT1` as a potential candidate for better-performing SB-VAWT supporting struts. (source: sources/vj21.md)
