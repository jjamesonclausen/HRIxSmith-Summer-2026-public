## Blade Material

This `vj21` paper treats blade material as one of the most critical design choices for smaller-capacity straight-bladed VAWTs because it controls fatigue life, weight, corrosion resistance, manufacturability, and cost. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Parameter

- The source says the blade material should combine high yield strength, fatigue resistance, stiffness, low density, corrosion resistance, and low-cost manufacturability. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It reviews wood/wood-epoxy, aluminum, and fiberglass-reinforced composite options, and it references a broader six-material comparison that also included stainless steel, low-carbon steel, and titanium. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The paper emphasizes fatigue as the major recurring material problem for both HAWTs and VAWTs. (source: sources/vj21.md)

## Outcome

- The paper says aluminum is common but limited by poor fatigue properties in dynamic wind-turbine service. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It describes wood as low-cost with good fatigue behavior but limited by moisture stability and joining efficiency. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The source says fiberglass-reinforced composites offer low density, good mechanical properties, corrosion resistance, property tailorability, and versatile fabrication methods. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It reports that pultruded FRP was found economically more viable than the other materials considered in the cited comparison study. (source: sources/vj21.md)
