## Blade-Wake Interaction

Interaction between an advancing VAWT blade and the wake left by another blade or by earlier revolutions of the rotor. (source: sources/va23.md)

- The `va23` paper treats blade-wake interaction (`BWI`) as one of the main reasons Darrieus troposkien VAWTs lose torque and power. (source: sources/va23.md)
- Its explanation is that the wake vortices modify the incoming angle of attack on the advancing blade, which usually reduces lift and therefore reduces torque and power. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The source says these detrimental effects depend on tip-speed ratio. (source: sources/va23.md)
- In the same paper, the low-TSR rising part of the `Cp` curve is associated more with dynamic stall behavior, while the post-peak drop is associated more with secondary effects including BWI. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The shifted-troposkien designs are proposed specifically to reduce direct wake interference between the two blades. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The paper reports that partial vertical shifting (`50% STS-VAWT`) improves performance more than the baseline, while the fully shifted case shows that reducing BWI alone is not enough if blade size and solidity are reduced too much. (source: sources/va23.md)
