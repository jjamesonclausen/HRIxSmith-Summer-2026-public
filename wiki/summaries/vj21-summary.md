## vj21 Summary

This paper is a design-parameter analysis for smaller-capacity fixed-pitch straight-bladed VAWTs rather than a report on one new built turbine. It organizes the main choices that drive cost-effectiveness, startup, efficiency, structural behavior, and practical deployment. (source: sources/vj21.md)

- The source lists `20` design parameters, spanning blade shape, blade number, struts, swept area, solidity, aspect ratio, chord-radius ratio, rated and cut-in/cut-out speeds, TSR, rotor speed, pitching, braking, loads, materials, noise, and aesthetics. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It argues that the most sensitive parameters include blade airfoil, supporting-strut configuration and shape, solidity, and blade material. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The paper favors asymmetric thick low-speed airfoils over the commonly used symmetric NACA 4-digit airfoils for smaller fixed-pitch SB-VAWTs, citing better startup and low-speed performance. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- It reports that high solidity helps low-speed torque but raises cost, narrows the efficient TSR band, and increases inertia. (source: sources/vj21.md)
- The source also recommends avoiding very low aspect ratio, notes that three blades smooth force and torque fluctuations better than two, and identifies pultruded FRP as the most economically viable blade material among the compared options. (source: sources/vj21.md)

> Uncertainty: several sections discuss candidate ideas like winglets, endplates, and elliptical blades as promising, but the paper explicitly says further aerodynamic and economic analysis is still required before judging their practical merit. (source: sources/vj21.md)
