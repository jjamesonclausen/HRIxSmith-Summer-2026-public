## va9 Source Summary

Summary of `sources/va9.md`. (source: sources/va9.md)

This paper presents a self-starting urban Darrieus VAWT design based on an EN0005 blade profile, a blade-end design, performance-prediction modeling, and field tests. (source: sources/va9.md)

Key points:
- The paper frames Darrieus startup as a central problem and lists external electricity feed-in, guide vanes, hybrid Savonius-Darrieus configurations, blade pitch optimization, blade form optimization, and specific blade-profile design as ways researchers have tried to solve it. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The EN0005 profile is designed so the upper surface acts as a high-lift surface, the first 20% of the lower surface is also high-lift, and the remaining lower surface finishes in a cup form that increases drag when the turbine is stopped in the downstream zone. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The authors compare EN0005 against NACA0018, NACA0020, NACA4418, and NACA4420 using pressure-coefficient contributions to tangential and normal force. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The paper reports EN0005 has better self-start capability, better lift coefficient between angles -60 degrees and -10 degrees, lower drag coefficient in that same interval, and a higher moment coefficient peak between -30 degrees and 0 degrees compared with the compared profiles. (source: sources/va9.md)
- For performance prediction, the paper describes single streamtube, multiple streamtube, double-multiple streamtube, vortex, and cascade models, then proposes a sliced DMS approach for complex blade forms and skewed-flow cases. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The DMS prediction case uses a 4.6 m high, 2 m radius, five-bladed straight VAWT with 0.30 m profile chord at V∞ = 12 m/s and reports EN0005 gives better high-TSR performance than NACA0012 and NACA0018. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The field-test prototype dimensions are 36.0 cm blade body height, 48.0 cm rotor height, 17.3 cm rotor radius, and 5.3 cm blade-profile chord. (source: sources/va9.md)
- The field tests report self-start at 1.25 m/s, stable behavior in a wind-tunnel stress test at 25 m/s, and no audible noise emission in the tested urban environment. (source: sources/va9.md)
