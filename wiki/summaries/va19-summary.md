## va19 Summary

MIT student feasibility study on small-scale rooftop wind power that combines campus measurements, long-term airport correlation, and cost-benefit analysis to rank candidate buildings and turbine options. (source: sources/va19.md)

![Source figure](va19-fig7.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 7. Average predicted and measured wind speeds and classification. [[va19|Source]]
![Source figure](va19-fig19.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 19. Payback versus a carbon tax or credit for a Skystream on Eastgate. [[va19|Source]]

Key points:
- The report says most MIT buildings tested were Class I wind sites, while Eastgate alone reached Class II and ranked as the strongest candidate in its dataset. (source: sources/va19.md)
- It compares two commercial small turbines for campus use: the `Skystream 3.7` and the parapet-oriented `AeroVironment AVX1000`. (source: sources/va19.md)
- Using long-term correlation with Beverly Municipal Airport data, the authors conclude that a `Skystream 3.7` on Eastgate could produce electricity for about `$0.08/kWh` and pay back within turbine lifetime. (source: sources/va19.md)
- The same source says no `AVX1000` scenario came close to economic feasibility, with even the best case around `90 years` payback. (source: sources/va19.md)
- It also reports strong MIT undergraduate support for both carbon-emission reduction and on-campus wind-turbine installation. (source: sources/va19.md)
