## va17 Summary

MIT campus rooftop-wind siting thesis that uses CFD to compare Eastgate dormitory and Johnson Athletic Center as candidates for small building-mounted wind turbines. (source: sources/va17.md)

![Source figure](va17-fig2.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 2. Diagram showing wind acceleration over edge of building. [[va17|Source]]
![Source figure](va17-fig12.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 12. Region of peak wind velocity surrounding Johnson Athletic Center. [[va17|Source]]

Key points:
- The thesis frames architectural wind turbines as edge-mounted rooftop systems that try to use Venturi acceleration over a building edge. (source: sources/va17.md)
- Using prior MIT site data, the CFD cases were based on a prevailing wind direction of 205 degrees SSW and an average free-flow wind speed of 4.9 m/s. (source: sources/va17.md)
- In the Eastgate model, the maximum wind speed was 5.5 m/s, about 13% above the incident wind speed, but the peak region sat about 15 m above the roof rather than at the roof edge. (source: sources/va17.md)
- In the Johnson Athletic Center model, the reported peak wind speed was 6.3 m/s, about 29% above the incident wind speed, with the high-speed region near the building edge about 5 m above the roof. (source: sources/va17.md)
- The thesis recommends follow-up anemometer measurements on the west roof edge of Johnson Athletic Center instead of treating the CFD output as final proof of site suitability. (source: sources/va17.md)
- It also notes that fully offsetting Johnson Athletic Center's energy use with small turbines would require hundreds of machines, so the practical case is partial offset plus demonstration value rather than full building supply. (source: sources/va17.md)
