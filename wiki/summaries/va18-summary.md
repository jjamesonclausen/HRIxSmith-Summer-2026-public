## va18 Summary

Urban wind-resource assessment paper for the MIT campus that combines met-tower measurements, CFD, and multiple climatology-transfer methods to compare two candidate small-turbine sites. (source: sources/va18.md)

Key points:
- The paper treats dense urban geometry as a micro-meteorological problem where building-induced acceleration, channeling, blocking, recirculation, and turbulence can change wind resource substantially over short distances. (source: sources/va18.md)
- Of the two MIT sites, the western site `MT2` is reported as the stronger resource because it sits in a high-wind channel through Brigg's field, while the eastern site `MT1` is in a more turbulent building wake. (source: sources/va18.md)
- The UrbaWind CFD setup used 1 m by 1 m resolution near the areas of interest, about 4 to 5 million cells, and 12 directional computations with added refinement near the prevailing 280 degree wind direction. (source: sources/va18.md)
- The source compares direct observations, 13-year MCP normalization, local climatology assimilation, background climatology assimilation, and TopoWind-assisted climatology transfer; all methods agree that `MT2` outperforms `MT1`. (source: sources/va18.md)
- The paper says local climatology assimilation matched the measured site-to-site ratios best, with simulated speed and power ratios of 1.11 and 1.35 versus measured ratios of 1.10 and 1.42. (source: sources/va18.md)
