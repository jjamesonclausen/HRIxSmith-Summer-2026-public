## vj3 Source Summary

Summary of `sources/vj3.md`. (source: sources/vj3.md)

This article explains how atmospheric turbulence affects wind energy performance, turbine loading, wake behavior, and noise propagation. It emphasizes that turbulence is not a single-direction effect: it can help or hurt power production depending on wind speed, shear, and atmospheric stability. (source: sources/vj3.md)

Key points:
- Turbulence intensity is defined as horizontal wind-speed standard deviation divided by mean wind speed over a 10-minute period. (source: sources/vj3.md)
- TKE captures all three velocity components and is often a better measure than turbulence intensity alone. (source: sources/vj3.md)
- Turbulence is generated by convection, shear, friction, and obstacles, and it varies by time of day and terrain. (source: sources/vj3.md)
- High turbulence can lower power near rated speed but improve performance near cut-in speed. (source: sources/vj3.md)
- Turbine wakes increase downwind loads and can affect microclimates and noise. (source: sources/vj3.md)
