## va23 Summary

Wind-tunnel comparison of a conventional two-bladed troposkien Darrieus VAWT against two novel shifted-troposkien variants intended to reduce blade-wake interaction while keeping the same swept area. (source: sources/va23.md)

![Source figure](va23-fig1.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 1. Vertical axis wind turbine (VAWT) configurations: (A) conventional VAWT (troposkien shape), (B) novel 50% STS-VAWT (50% shifted troposkien shape-VAWT), (C) novel 100% STS-VAWT. [[va23|Source]]
![Source figure](va23-fig9.jpg)
Original caption: Figure 9. Power coefficient (Cp) vs TSR after corrections at 600 rpm. [[va23|Source]]

Key points:
- The paper compares three `2`-bladed NACA0015 troposkien-family turbines with the same overall height, swept area, radius of rotation, and chord length: a conventional baseline, a `50%` shifted troposkien shape, and a `100%` shifted troposkien shape. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The novel shifted concepts try to improve performance by reducing blade-wake interaction, while also shortening blade length and reducing blade material. (source: sources/va23.md)
- At `600 rpm`, the `50% STS-VAWT` shows the best corrected peak performance in the paper, about `Cp = 0.23`, roughly `10%` above the conventional turbine's corrected peak near `Cp = 0.21`. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The `100% STS-VAWT` reduces blade-wake interaction further, but the paper says its smaller blade size and lower solidity offset that benefit and keep it below the `50%` shifted case. (source: sources/va23.md)
- The paper also proposes a modified Pope-and-Harper blockage correction using local velocity-ratio measurements, because the raw tunnel blockage ratio is about `19.1%`. (source: sources/va23.md)
