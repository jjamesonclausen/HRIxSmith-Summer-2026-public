## Solwind Vertical Axis Wind Turbine

The Solwind turbine is described as a New Zealand vertical-axis turbine designed for low startup speed, quiet operation, and remote-site power generation. (source: sources/va3.md)

## Geometry

- The source shows two- and four-rotor-blade versions. (source: sources/va3.md)
- The blades are made from composite fiberglass, stainless steel, and lightweight aluminum. (source: sources/va3.md)
- The generator is a low-speed Magnetic Levitation Alternator with only one moving part. (source: sources/va3.md)
- The generator is base-mounted at the foot of the mast, with no slipping assembly, dolly bearing assembly, brush assembly, or power cables running up the mast. (source: sources/va3.md)
- Stayed single-pole mast assemblies are designed for remote-area erection without extra lifting gear; mast sections use high-tensile steel and standard 6.5 m lengths. (source: sources/va3.md)

## Performance

- The turbine is designed to start up at 1.5 m/s. (source: sources/va3.md)
- It starts producing power at 3.7 m/s. (source: sources/va3.md)
- It produces rated output at 10 m/s. (source: sources/va3.md)
- At high wind speeds the turbine stalls and slows around 27 m/s. (source: sources/va3.md)
- Maximum design wind speed is 190 km/hr. (source: sources/va3.md)
- The source says the design is extremely quiet because the blades remain the same distance from the mast and avoid the coning noise of conventional HAWTs. (source: sources/va3.md)
