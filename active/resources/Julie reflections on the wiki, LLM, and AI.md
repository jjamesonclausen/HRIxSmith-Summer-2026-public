# Julie's Reflections on the Wiki, LLM, and AI

This note extracts Julie's project reflections and opinions from the daily log. It is organized by theme and date rather than as a chronological work summary. (source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

## The Wiki Should Support Real Understanding

### Making the system useful, not merely complete

- Julie noticed that an LLM quiz was emphasizing small details, such as build materials, rather than overall concepts, and questioned how to correct that focus. (2026-06-29; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She thought the LLM's VAWT-focused organizing instruction was a useful start, but was unsure whether it would meaningfully change the wiki without examples of a good organization system. (2026-07-01; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She saw potential in automatically linking terms such as tip-speed ratio to their concept or method pages, but worried that linking every mention could make connections less useful. (2026-07-01; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She considered using tags for key design choices so Obsidian could reveal their connections, and considered an equations page, while explicitly questioning whether the latter would be applicable. (2026-07-01; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

### The wiki as an accessible research partner

- Julie described the LLM wiki's main benefit as having an apparent field expert that could synthesize many trusted sources and answer questions on demand. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She valued the ability to ask whether an idea could be supported or found in the collected material, rather than having to search every source manually. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

## LLM-Assisted Decisions Need Scrutiny

### Value of structured comparison

- Julie found the LLM very useful for rapidly comparing information across candidate designs. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She found weighted decision tables particularly helpful for comparing designs. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- The team used multiple perspectives, including an experienced professional, a novice designer with limited CAD experience, and their own rankings, then compared the top results from each test. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- When discussing ranking approaches, Julie raised the distinction between qualitative and quantitative outputs and asked whether an LLM's rankings or numbers could be trusted, especially when data are missing. (2026-07-07; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

### Speed can hide weak understanding

- Julie was struck that the team could narrow designs quickly with the LLM, but worried that this could bypass the struggle with technical concepts through which learning often happens. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She also saw a possible counterpoint: because the LLM can synthesize trusted sources, it might provide a broader overall picture of the concepts than a conventional, more isolated research process. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- Julie's reflection presents this as an unresolved tradeoff, not a conclusion that fast LLM-assisted design selection is automatically reliable. (source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

## AI Tools Are Promising but Not Automatically Reliable

### CAD-generation concerns

- Julie saw ZooKeeper as promising because it could model a whole turbine quickly, but worried that its underlying structure might not be solid enough for later edits. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- During early modeling, she found that asking the tool to change one element could alter parts that were already acceptable. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She was frustrated by the inability to manually edit the result directly and asked the tool to identify the code lines it changed so she could make adjustments herself. (2026-07-10; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

### AI output requires validation

- Julie documented repeated ingestion problems: missing pictures, equations, or metadata; unwanted summarization; and formatting errors. (2026-06-29 to 2026-07-06; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She found that the LLM did not appear to know much about AI capabilities within SimScale when she questioned it. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- Her CFD work included difficulty verifying simulation results and uncertainty about the prior team's reported setup, showing that a simulation result could not simply be accepted without checking. (2026-07-13; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She also recorded that one model was fast overall but had trouble with pictures, a practical reminder that tool performance can vary by task. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

## The Project Is Also About Learning How to Learn With AI

- Julie described the eventual deliverable as a user manual for future teams on how to use an LLM or other AI tool to learn and support a project, rather than only producing an excellent final turbine. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She recognized that AI tools can let a student take a back seat unless learning is made intentional. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- Her proposed response was to make the tools teach and quiz the user, rather than only providing outputs. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- She framed the project-specific learning as learning to set up and use a wiki, Obsidian, VS Code, and AI tools in ways tailored to the team's needs: learning how to learn from the tools. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)
- Julie also noted a desire to make LLM decision-making easier to inspect through a visual decision matrix and requests for several diverse answers. (2026-07-15; source: active/_Julie_daily_log.md)

## Potential Uses in a Blog Post

- Frame the wiki as infrastructure for asking better questions, not a replacement for technical judgment.
- Use the tension between fast synthesis and slower, durable learning as the central reflection.
- Include the weighted design screening as an example of a helpful LLM workflow, then explain why rankings still need evidence checks and human review.
- Use CAD and CFD difficulties to show why AI-generated artifacts and recommendations must be validated rather than accepted at face value.
